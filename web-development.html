Questions to ask before building

Who is the application for?

Define the main users and the tasks they need to complete. A clear audience helps shape permissions, navigation, and the level of guidance each screen needs.

What should it connect to?

List current systems, data sources, and any required integrations. Their access, capabilities, and limitations can influence the project scope and delivery plan.

A practical example

Turn a scattered enquiry process into one clear journey.

Imagine a customer requesting a service online. Instead of following up across email and spreadsheets, a web application could collect the enquiry, show its status, and give staff a shared view of the next action. The exact workflow is shaped around your business and existing tools.

For the customer

A straightforward form, a confirmation, and a place to find relevant updates or information.

For the team

A structured record, clear ownership, and a view of enquiries that need attention.

For the business

More consistent information and an opportunity to identify bottlenecks in the process.

For the next phase

A foundation that can be reviewed and extended as usage and priorities become clearer.

Built around useful foundations

The parts users may never notice still matter.

Behind a smooth web experience are decisions about access, data, validation, and maintenance. These foundations help the product behave consistently as more people use it and more of the business depends on it.

  • Clear roles and permissions for different users
  • Useful validation and feedback for forms and actions
  • Responsive layouts for desktop, tablet, and mobile use
  • A maintainable structure for future changes

A collaborative process

Your team brings the context. We help shape the product.

The strongest brief does not need every technical answer. A description of the current process, the people involved, and the frustrating workarounds is enough to start a useful conversation.

Together, we can clarify what belongs in the first release, identify dependencies such as content or third-party access, and make trade-offs visible before they become surprises. The result is a more grounded scope and a shared view of what the application is meant to improve.

You do not need a complete specification to begin. A few examples of the current process can help identify the users, decisions, and business rules the web application needs to support.

A note on integrations and scope

Connecting an application to an external platform depends on that platform’s API, account access, documentation, and usage terms. We review those dependencies during discovery so integration effort, required access, and any third-party costs can be discussed before implementation.
