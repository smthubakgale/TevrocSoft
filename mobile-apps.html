More than a screen size

Plan the experience from first open to everyday use.

Mobile products work best when their purpose is clear and the important tasks are easy to reach. We help shape the experience around how customers or staff will actually use it, including what happens when they are on the move or briefly offline.

Map the key journeys

Prioritise the main tasks—such as booking, service updates, or field reporting—before adding secondary features.

Choose an approach

Compare native and cross-platform options in context, taking device features, maintenance, and the intended audience into account.

Think beyond launch

Plan for testing, feedback, platform requirements, content changes, and future improvements as the product evolves.

Mobile app questions, answered

Should we build native or cross-platform?

There is no universal answer. The choice depends on the features you need, target devices, timelines, maintenance plans, and the experience expected by your users. We compare these factors before recommending a direction.

What happens after the first release?

Apps need ongoing attention as operating systems, devices, and user needs change. We can plan testing, release preparation, and a practical way to prioritise follow-up improvements.

A product people return to

Make the important action easy to find.

A mobile app competes with interruptions, small screens, and changing connectivity. Prioritising a few high-value tasks can make the experience feel lighter and easier to learn than trying to fit every business function into the first release.

Customer app example

Help a customer make a booking, check an update, or find the information they need without navigating a complex menu.

Field team example

Give staff a practical way to capture job details, refer to instructions, or record progress while away from a desk.

Respect the device

Account for screen sizes, touch targets, permissions, battery, and the platform conventions people already know.

Design helpful states

Plan loading, empty, offline, and error states so the app remains understandable when conditions are not ideal.

Designed for real-world conditions

Make the experience dependable beyond the demo.

A mobile product has to work across devices, contexts, and interruptions. Planning for the everyday realities early helps the app stay useful when a user is busy, on the move, or dealing with a weak connection.

Permissions with purpose

Request location, camera, or notification access only when it supports a clear feature, and explain why it is needed.

Useful offline behavior

Decide which information should remain available and how pending changes should be handled when service returns.

Clear progress and recovery

Show what is happening during longer tasks and give people a practical way to retry or continue after an error.

From first use to repeat use

Make each step feel clear on a small screen.

Mobile screens leave less room for uncertainty. Helpful hierarchy, concise language, and predictable feedback let people focus on their task instead of working out how the interface behaves.

Comfortable interactions

Design tap targets and forms for touch, with enough spacing and feedback to avoid accidental actions.

Respectful notifications

Use notifications only where they help, and give people control over optional updates.

Thoughtful connectivity

Set expectations for slow connections, retries, and any data users may need to access offline.
