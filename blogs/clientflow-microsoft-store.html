The Windows release

One desktop. A lot less bouncing around.

ClientFlow has landed on Microsoft Store, making the Windows edition easier to find and install. Inside, product records, fulfillment, sales conversations, and business app projects share one workspace—so the workday can feel less like a tour of disconnected tabs.

Take a closer look at the screens below: start with the catalog, follow an order’s journey, answer a customer, then switch gears to a business app project. Different jobs, one place to pick up the thread.

Whether you are running a shop, coordinating orders, or helping a customer find the right product, the Windows release gives these workflows room on a larger screen. Here is a look at how the pieces connect.

Products and inventory

First stop: the product shelf

The Products page puts the catalog up front, with routes to related work like payments, point of sale, inventory, and suppliers close by. Each card brings an item’s image, name, SKU, price, and stock into one glance. Find what you sell, check the details, and keep moving through the tasks that make a sale happen.

That shared view is handy when product information is part of a bigger conversation: a team can refer to the same catalog while moving between the tools that support the order.

Orders and fulfillment

From “still packing” to “delivered”

The dashboard lays out the delivery journey: awaiting packing, awaiting collection, in transit, and delivered. Shipping revenue, costs, and failed deliveries get their own callouts, while charts make room to spot movement without opening every order one by one. The example shows zero-value sample data, so it’s a tour of the dashboard—not a live business report.

When orders are moving through several stages, a shared overview can help a team decide where to look next—whether that is a package awaiting collection or a delivery that needs follow-up.

Sales conversations

A customer question, right where the team can help

Products, Customers, the WhatsApp inbox, Product finder, and Sales scanner all have a place in the Sales workspace. The selected conversation shows a business-selection prompt and the reply that follows. The useful bit? The team can see the conversation while they work, instead of chasing context across separate screens.

Keeping the thread beside the sales tools makes it easier to understand what the customer has already asked and which business they want help with before composing a response.

App projects

Keep your business app ideas moving

App Projects gives owners a quick project overview and a way back into a selected project’s editor. There’s also a serious note among the controls: a project link contains a private access key, and anyone holding it may access connected data. Treat that link like a credential—share it carefully and rotate it if it may have escaped the right hands.

It is a helpful reminder when projects are shared across a team: convenience is great, but access links should go only to the people who need them.

Customer-facing projects

Step into the storefront before your customers do

This preview opens on a furniture storefront, complete with announcement bar, navigation, collection search, and a promotional hero. It’s a customer-facing page inside the project workflow—not an inventory screen—and a glimpse of how ClientFlow projects can connect back-office work with the experience a business puts online.

That connection is useful for businesses that need both a place to manage operations and a way to shape the pages customers see. The preview makes the storefront part of the same project story.

Project development

Behind the preview: files, code, and a running project

Here’s the workshop behind the storefront: a project file tree, an open configuration file, and terminal output as the preview starts and business data loads. This is the supporting development environment, not another management screen—and it shows the edit-and-preview context around a business app project.

Seeing the preview alongside its project files helps distinguish the customer-facing result from the tools used to build and check it.
