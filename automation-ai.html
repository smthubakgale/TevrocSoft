Start with the process

Not every task needs AI. Every automation needs a reason.

We look for repetitive steps, avoidable hand-offs, and information that has to be copied between tools. From there, we can decide whether a straightforward rule, an integration, or an AI-assisted step is appropriate.

Map the current flow

Understand who does each step, what information they need, and where delays or rework occur.

Define boundaries

Decide which actions are safe to automate, what needs approval, and how exceptions are handled.

Review the outcome

Agree on practical indicators such as fewer repeated steps, clearer status, or faster routing.

Keep people in the loop

Automation should make routine work easier to manage, not hide important decisions. Workflows can be designed to surface exceptions, request human approval, and provide a clear record of what happened—depending on the systems and scope involved.

Build in manageable steps

Starting with a focused workflow makes it easier to validate the process and learn from real usage. Additional steps or integrations can then be considered with a clearer understanding of their value and dependencies.

Automation questions, answered

How do we choose a first workflow?

Look for a repeated process with understandable rules, a clear owner, and an outcome you can observe. Start small enough to test the exceptions and learn before expanding it.

Will AI make every decision automatically?

That is not the goal. The workflow should define when AI may assist, what needs human review, how uncertainty is handled, and which actions remain under your team’s control.

Example workflow

Make a request easier to receive, review, and resolve.

For example, a new request could be captured in a consistent format, assigned a category, and sent to the right queue. AI might help classify unstructured text, while a person confirms exceptions or decisions with greater impact.

01 · Trigger

A request arrives

A defined event starts the workflow, such as a form submission or a record update.

02 · Interpret

Prepare the information

Rules or AI can organise the input into fields the process can use.

03 · Check

Handle uncertainty

Validate required details and send incomplete or sensitive cases for review.

04 · Route

Move to the next step

Notify the right person or system and retain enough context for follow-up.

Data and access

Know what information enters the workflow.

Automation often moves information between people and systems. Before implementation, identify which data is required, where it comes from, who may access it, and how long it needs to be retained.

For AI-assisted work, also agree which content can be used to prepare a response or recommendation. Data handling, privacy, and provider terms should be considered against your organisation’s obligations.

Measure the useful change

Look for better work, not just more automation.

A workflow is valuable when it improves a meaningful part of the process. Depending on the use case, you might review the number of manual hand-offs, time spent on repeated entry, the completeness of requests, or how often a person needs to correct an automated step.

These indicators help the team decide whether the workflow should be adjusted, extended, or kept deliberately simple.

Turn uncertainty into an explicit rule

Design the exception path before the automation goes live.

Reliable automation is not just the flow that succeeds. It also accounts for missing fields, unavailable services, unexpected inputs, and requests that should be reviewed by a person.

A

Detect

Check whether required information is present and whether the request falls within the workflow’s intended scope.

B

Pause or retry

Use a controlled retry or ask for clarification when it is safe and useful to do so.

C

Escalate with context

Send exceptions to an appropriate person with enough information to investigate and continue.

Keep a person accountable for important outcomes

AI suggestions can be incomplete or incorrect. For decisions that affect customers, finances, access, or sensitive records, define review and approval requirements with the responsible people in your organisation. Automation can support the process without replacing the accountability around it.
